# Fiction Diagnostic Audit

Use the relevant sections for revision, critique, “sounds generic” requests, and final draft checks. Apply only checks that fit the requested outcome, scale, and genre. For wording, voice, or local edits, inspect the affected passage and relevant continuity constraints; audit structure only when a structural defect affects the requested outcome.

## Structural gates

Write every result down, including zero findings.

| Gate | Test | Failure signal | Useful intervention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stated meaning | Quote narration or dialogue that explains the story's theme | The same meaning already exists in action, image, or consequence | Cut the explanation; strengthen the event if meaning vanishes |
| Live uncertainty | Stop near 75% and list three plausible endings | Only one ending remains, or the planned ending is first | Delay confirmation or restore an alternate interpretation |
| Disclosure | List major answers and where each became certain | Answers arrive well before their consequences finish | Move certainty later; preserve clue fairness |
| Choice cost | Write the best case for the rejected option | The best case is weak or obviously immoral | Give the alternative real value, constituency, or loss |
| Pressure curve | Score scenes or chapters 1-5 in order | Flat line or clean staircase | Add release, reversal, delay, or a different pressure source |
| Recontextualization | Name what an important reveal changes about an earlier scene | It changes only future action | Seed an earlier detail with two fair readings |
| Causal texture | Mark who or what causes the outcome | Every line serves the main plot and focal agency solves everything | Let secondary agendas, institutions, chance, or timing matter |
| Thread closure | List open and closed threads | Everything closes, or residue is accidental | Choose what must resolve and what remains productively live |
| Moral polarity | State the strongest case against the focal character | Opposition has no credible case | Add cost, complicity, or a legitimate conflicting good |
| Contract | Name the promised genre satisfaction | A diagnostic “fix” removes the payoff | Restore the promise and place complexity elsewhere |

## Emotion channel audit

For a representative passage, tag each emotional rendering:

- `NAMED`: the emotion is stated plainly
- `BODY`: pulse, breath, chest, jaw, stomach, temperature, tears
- `ACTION`: decision, avoidance, mistake, fixation, displacement
- `SPEECH`: dialogue, silence, evasion, misdirection
- `INTERPRETATION`: narrator explains what the feeling means
- `MIRROR`: weather or setting performs the emotion

No ratio is universally correct. Flag clustering: repeated BODY and MIRROR tags with few other channels usually indicate automatic “showing.” Naming an emotion can be the less mannered choice. INTERPRETATION should add character bias or new information, not translate what the scene already showed.

## Specificity and default audit

List every conspicuous name, profession, setting, object, weather image, and revelation.

Ask:

1. Could this choice have come from the first plausible completion of the premise?
2. Does it arise from place, class, era, work, relationship, or prior canon?
3. Does it create consequences, or merely decorate?
4. Has the manuscript already spent a close variant?
5. Did revision replace a precise odd fact with a grander generic one?

Representative high-collision inventions include lighthouses and keepers; names such as Elias, Mara, and Elara; clockmakers, librarians, bakers, fishermen, mayors, and conductors; memory-preserving machines; found letters; mysterious shops; and “the last of” something. These are not banned. Their value must come from the story rather than their atmosphere.

## Continuity and voice audit

For continuations, compare the new unit with the source:

- viewpoint, tense, and psychic distance
- character knowledge boundaries
- current desires and relationship states
- causal consequences still in force
- diction and sentence movement
- realism and genre boundary
- figurative domains and images already spent

A cadence match does not excuse a canon or genre violation.

## Prose pass

Run after any needed structural revision; skip structural revision when the request and evidence do not require it.

Quote and assess:

- assistant framing before or after the fiction
- aphoristic paragraph closers and a final line that explains the lesson
- symmetrical tricolons, mirror sentences, or repeated rhetorical pivots
- synonym cycling for the same person or object
- significance inflation instead of concrete consequence
- generic sensory bundles and embodied shorthand
- weather that merely duplicates mood
- uniform sentence and paragraph movement
- explanatory recaps at openings, breaks, and endings

Do not enforce punctuation or vocabulary quotas. A deliberate phrase is not a defect because it appears on a list. Look for clusters, unearned repetition, and mismatch with the established voice.

## Review format

When the user requests critique rather than revision, report:

```text
LAYER       structural | continuity | voice | prose
PATTERN     diagnostic name
EVIDENCE    exact quotation or counted sequence
EFFECT      what it does to tension, character, meaning, or voice
MOVE        concise revision direction
```

Rank structural and continuity findings before prose findings. Do not infer or score authorship. These patterns may weaken a draft regardless of how it was produced.

## Recovery

- If a gate conflicts with a genre promise, keep the promise.
- If a pattern is clearly deliberate and earned, note it and leave it.
- If fixing a line exposes a missing scene function that affects the requested outcome, re-derive the scene rather than filling the gap with new explanation.
- If the user asked for findings only, do not rewrite.
